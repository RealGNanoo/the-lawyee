When the Lawyees Awaken: Will the Tables Turn?

The Lexicon of Power

Lawyee noun | /lɔɪˈiː/

A member of the general population who is subject to legal systems and instruments, but who holds no active role, influence, or participatory agency in the creation, shaping, or administration of those laws.

Lawyer noun | /ˈlɔɪ.jər/

An individual or entity — operating through formal state machinery, transnational institutions, economic leverage, administrative authority, doctrinal discourse, or informal community structures — that actively participates in the creation, interpretation, administration, or protection of the legal and normative rules that govern society.

This expansive category encompasses state professionals (legislators, judges, advocates, law enforcement personnel, and administrative bureaucrats), supranational governance bodies and international financial institutions (such as multilateral organizations, global regulatory agencies, and international courts), financial and commercial elites (corporate entities and market lobbies), doctrinal and institutional architects (legal academics, policy think-tanks, and private standard-setting bodies), and spiritual or traditional authorities (religious leaders, village elders, and customary arbiters) who shape the architecture of social control.

The Broken Compact

Political philosophers have argued for centuries that a legitimate legal order rests on a simple premise: the people bound by the law should have a hand in writing it. Thinkers from Rousseau onward imagined a social contract where lawgivers and law-takers meet in a shared civic space.

In practice, though? That alignment almost never happens.

Instead, we live with a massive structural divide. A tiny minority of lawyers—spread across parliaments, corporate boardrooms, international tribunals, and bureaucratic desks—hold exclusive custody over the legal pen. Meanwhile, the lawyees make up the vast majority of humanity. We navigate a legal system built entirely by others, one that shapes everything from our daily routines to our long-term security.

Sometimes the law protects us, and sometimes it binds us. It might offer economic opportunity or impose a heavy burden. But whether the outcome feels fair or restrictive, we experience these instruments as passive recipients rather than active participants. Most of the time, we just accept it.

The Inevitable Reckoning

Can an asymmetry that deep last forever?

Look back over the last hundred years. History is littered with iron-clad power structures that looked completely unshakeable — until they weren't. Think of how Gandhi mobilized the masses, turning ordinary, voiceless subjects into active agents of history who could dismantle an empire through sheer collective agency. Think of the rapid collapse of global empires after World War II, or the breathtaking speed with which Soviet communism dissolved when people simply stopped believing in the script handed down to them.

Power always looks permanent from the inside. But when a population wakes up to its structural reality — when passive compliance slowly shifts into actual agency — the floor drops out from under legal hegemony.

The tables will turn. The only real question left is when.
